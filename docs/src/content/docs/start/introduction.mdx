Holtdroid is a self-hosted web portal that launches Android devices on demand
for testing APKs. It is built for teams whose builds land in a chat channel
and whose apps lean on WebViews: QA engineers, web developers working on
hybrid apps, support engineers reproducing a customer bug.

## Why it exists

I'm a frontend engineer, and I build Holtdroid for my own use first. Most
users of the products I've worked on open them inside an app's embedded
WebView or in Chrome, and a fair share of them do it on old potato phones 🥔
with cursed combinations of Android and [WebView versions](/concepts/webview-versions/). Their bugs only reproduce on
exactly those combinations.

I'm also very lazy, and debugging over ADB through Chrome DevTools is flaky
and awkward enough to drain whatever motivation was left. So instead of
fighting it I wrote a tool that starts the WebView I need and then digs into
what the page actually does: the [network requests](/session-tools/network/) on the critical loading path,
how well Cloudflare [caches](/session-tools/cache/) the assets, and more.

Maybe I'm the only weirdo out there who rewrites SVG filters by hand and loses
sleep over cache hit rates so that people on a bad connection get a page that
loads fast and doesn't cook their phone. Just in case I'm not, I made it public
^.^ If something should be fixed or added, I'd be glad to hear it: open an
[issue](https://github.com/holtdroid/holtdroid/issues).

## Getting around

The portal's navigation is a dock floating at the bottom of every page. From
left to right: the sections (**Apps**, and **App data**, **Pixel perfect**,
**Recordings** and **Computers** when they are enabled); a chip for each device
you are running, with a status dot (green: ready, blue: starting, amber: the
idle stop or the lifetime ends within 5 minutes, red: failed) and a hover card
with the device, its Android version, location, the idle countdown, **Open**
and **Stop**; **+** to launch an app; the admin pages (**Inventory**,
**Users**, **Audit log**); search; the documentation; the theme switch; and
your account menu. On smaller screens the admin pages, documentation and theme
fold into a **More** menu, and on narrow ones the sections too.

**⌘K** (Ctrl+K on Windows and Linux) anywhere in the portal opens a search
over your running devices (open or stop one), the apps (picking one opens its
launch dialog), recordings and pages, plus actions: upload an APK, switch the
theme, open the documentation. The documentation link carries the page you
were on in its address (`#returnUrl=`), so the documentation's own dock shows
**Open the portal**, which brings you back there, for as long as that tab
stays open.

## A session, end to end

1. Open the portal. The Apps page shows one row per app (package name) found
   in your configured sources (a folder or bucket that CI or a Slack sync job
   fills, plus your own uploads): its latest version, how many builds there
   are, which sources they come from and how many devices run it now; tabs
   filter by source and `/` jumps to the search. **Launch** on a row starts
   the latest build; the arrow at the end opens its builds (version, build
   number, branch, who posted it, size, age) with a launch button and a menu
   (download, origin, visibility, delete) each, and the app's name opens a page
   with every build's note and changelog. Builds of one version for different
   processors (x86 and ARM) share a row, and a launch takes the one the chosen
   device runs natively. Anyone signed in can give an app a description (what
   it is for, how to test it): it shows above the builds and, shortened, in
   the app's row. Pin the apps you test most: they come first in the list for
   everyone. Next to the list, the **Devices** card shows each provider's
   slots as a bar of ticks (running, starting, about to stop, free) and the
   running sessions, yours first; **Recent recordings** lists the newest
   recordings when recording is on.
2. Press **Launch**. The launch dialog puts the device together on the left:
   where it runs (a virtual device or a physical phone, each with how many are
   free, the virtual one preselected when the build runs on it), the Android
   version (versions below the app's `minSdk` are greyed out), the screen
   (favourites first, **All devices** opens a searchable picker), the WebView,
   the VPN location and app data. The right column previews the chosen phone
   with its size and cutout, sums up the choices and holds **Record from
   start** and **Launch**. A physical provider lists its phones instead,
   **Any available** by default. A fresh Android device boots (about a
   minute) while a progress card shows each real start stage and the time
   since launch. The ⚡ button next to Launch skips the dialog and launches
   with the provider and location you used last time (or the defaults). When
   every device is busy, or the one you picked, the launch waits in line and
   starts as soon as one frees up.
3. The APK is installed as-is and started with WebView debugging enabled at
   runtime.
4. The session page has a header, the **Device** panel on the left, the
   device's screen in the middle and the tools on the right: Chrome DevTools
   with a picker for the inspected target (any WebView page, iframe or
   worker in any process), Logcat, DNS, Routes, Files and Terminal, with
   WebSockets, Cache and Pixel perfect under **More**. The header shows the
   app and its build (click the name to switch to another build) with the
   device, Android, WebView and VPN location under it; the time left,
   **Extend** and **Stop** sit on its right. By default the screen's side
   follows the screen: it grows or shrinks until the screen fills its height
   or the side reaches 70% of the page. A landscape screen splits the page
   top and bottom instead, the screen above the tools. Dragging the divider
   sizes it by hand; the button on the divider turns following back on.

   The screen sits in a frame with a toolbar beside it (under it in
   landscape): Back, Home, Recent apps, the notifications and quick
   settings, Rotate (click turns left, shift-click right), Screenshot, the
   keyboard mode, Volume (the volume keys, the media volume slider and a
   tile that mutes Android itself), Power (locks the device or turns its
   screen off, with **Wake and unlock** and the key's mode in its panel) and
   **Restart app**. Rotate, Volume and Power open their panel on hover, on a
   right-click, a long press or the context-menu key; a panel stays open
   once you click inside it, until you click elsewhere or press Esc. When
   the device vibrates, the page shows a notification and plays a buzz.

   The Device panel holds the device's controls as rows in three tabs:
   **Environment** (Network, Location, Battery, Language, Time, Clipboard,
   Display), **Media** (Stream, Microphone, Camera, Recording) and **App**
   (App, Device, SSH). Each row shows a summary and its chevron opens it in
   place; the search button filters the rows of all tabs by name. A row
   whose settings differ from the defaults is marked **Changed**, its tab
   gets a dot, and the open row has a reset button.

   The Network row shows the VPN location, the external IP and the network
   conditions, with Wi-Fi, mobile data and airplane mode tiles, the VPN
   locations and a link to the network picker. On emulators with VPN
   locations the picker holds the emulated network conditions: slow or
   lossy networks and scenarios such as a subway ride, each with its
   description, and regions such as Bangladesh that add the round trips to
   CDN edges and to your servers there. The **DNS** tab lists every name the
   device looks up and rewrites names to your own addresses, with a TTL
   limit and saved sets of rewrites (see
   [DNS log and rewrites](/session-tools/dns/)), and **Routes** shows where
   every connection goes: each server's country and network, the location
   it left through, and far servers or connections that aren't reused (see
   [Routes](/session-tools/routes/)).

   The Stream row picks the video codec (H.264, the lightest for the server,
   by default; H.265 or AV1 where the browser decodes them), quality (the
   device's own up to 1080p, then 720p, 480p and 360p; a sharper screen is
   streamed at 1080p), frame rate (45 fps by default, up to the device's own
   and never above 60) and bitrate, switches the device audio (it plays in
   your browser by default, from your first click on the page unless the
   browser allows autoplay for the portal; until then the row says "Click
   anywhere for sound" and the Media tab gets a dot), shows the frames,
   bitrate and dropped frames the browser decodes right now, and switches
   the keyboard between text input and a virtual USB keyboard that types in
   any alphabet with the layout set on the device. The codec, the bitrate
   and a frame rate below 45 fps are remembered in the browser; quality and
   faster frame rates last for one session.

   The Clipboard row turns sync on or off with a click (both ways by
   default, or one direction) and keeps the last 10 synced texts, shown with
   · for spaces and ↵ for line breaks, each with buttons to copy it or save
   it as a text file. The Time row shows the device's clock and is marked
   while the clock is shifted, not in real time or in a time zone picked by
   hand; it has real-time sync, shifting the clock back or forward by any
   amount, an exact moment entered in your own time zone, and the time zone,
   which follows the VPN location unless you pick another one for the
   session. The Battery row shows the charge (65% and on battery at start)
   and has battery saver, charging and the charge level with presets. The
   Display row has a brightness slider and adaptive brightness (for this
   session only; the stream itself doesn't get darker, the device and its
   apps see the setting), the dark theme (light, dark or matching the
   portal's theme, which a device launched in that mode already has when the
   app first opens), Android's system navigation on emulators (gestures by
   default, or the back, home and recent apps buttons), the font size with a
   preview and the display size, Android's setting that scales buttons,
   icons and text by changing the density (the screen picked at launch keeps
   its resolution); rotation is there too. The Language row picks the system
   language and the keyboard layouts from a grid of flags: a click on a
   layout adds or removes it (up to 7, remembered in your browser and
   guessed from your keyboard where the browser allows). When SSH is on, the
   SSH row says whether a computer is connected to this device and lists the
   ADB clients and the ports your computers publish on the device, the `adb`
   command for this device on each connected computer, or the command that
   connects a computer. The App row restarts and reinstalls the app and
   saves its data; recording has its own row
   ([recordings](/concepts/recordings/)).

   On devices that can fake it, the **Location** row follows the VPN
   location until you tap the map, drag the dot, paste coordinates or use the
   locate button on the map for your own position. Under the map, **Route**
   walks, runs or drives the device through stops you tap on the map, along
   roads (or in straight lines) and optionally in a loop, speeding up, slowing for turns and
   drifting around the chosen pace; it keeps going until you stop it, even
   with the tab closed. Apps see the position move but no speed or bearing.

5. Walk away. After the idle timeout (30 minutes by default) the device is
   deleted and the slot frees up. The home page shows every active session,
   its owner and how long it has left.

## What it is not

- Not a device farm for automated test runs (yet). The contracts allow it,
  but the first goal is interactive debugging.
- Not a way to modify or re-sign APKs. Anything that needs repackaging is out
  of scope on purpose.
- Not a Play Store environment: sessions run redroid, a rooted AOSP
  userdebug build without Google services.

## Requirements

- A Kubernetes cluster (1.31+) with **x86_64 nodes with binder and a Mesa
  GPU** for sessions, dedicated to them. Each session takes about 1 GB of
  memory idle and a core while its screen streams.
- Permission to run Pods with extra capabilities (Pod Security level
  `privileged`) in Holtdroid's namespace, and a runtime with NRI
  (containerd 2.0+) on the session nodes.
- A forward-auth proxy in front of the ingress (Authelia, oauth2-proxy, …),
  unless you run it locally with authentication disabled.
- For builds from Slack: a Slack app with a bot token and a small sync job
  that copies them into a source.
